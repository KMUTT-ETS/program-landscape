## Abstract

The Curriculum Landscape application produces two quantitative outputs to inform decisions on the 297 academic programs at Khon Kaen University: a **Program Health Score** (a 0–100 composite across five operational dimensions) and a **Program Lifecycle Verdict** (a four-band classifier — Sunset / Stable / Rising / Frontier — that integrates the health score with content-modernity and AI-readiness signals). This paper specifies the inputs, formulas, aggregation rules, null-handling conventions, and governance guardrails of both outputs. The intended audience is decision-makers (University Council, executive officers, deans) and program coordinators who must interpret these scores when reviewing the program portfolio. The paper is deliberately conservative in its claims: every score informs, none decides, and program closure / merger / launch decisions remain a human-in-the-loop process requiring two-reviewer Council approval regardless of the computed values. Limitations and known data gaps are documented in §7.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Context

Khon Kaen University's Council and executive leadership routinely deliberate on the strategic positioning of the institution's 297 academic programs. Inputs to those deliberations have historically been (a) annually-reported enrollment and financial figures from internal divisions and (b) qualitative reviews authored by deans and program coordinators. Curriculum Landscape was commissioned to add a **third, quantitative layer**: a per-program health score and lifecycle classification computed reproducibly from operational data — TCAS recruitment statistics, registrar enrollment series, finance ledgers, HR rosters, faculty publication counts, demographic projections, and external labour-market signals.

The express intent is **not** to replace human judgement. Council members and deans bring institutional, political, and disciplinary context that no scoring system can encode. The intent is rather to provide a **defensible numeric anchor** that is consistent across programs and faculties, so that conversations about closure, merger, launch, and strategic investment start from a shared factual baseline.

### 1.2 Scope and audience

This paper specifies **two outputs**:

- The **Program Health Score** — a five-dimension composite that diagnoses *current operational state*.
- The **Program Lifecycle Verdict** — a four-band classifier that diagnoses *strategic trajectory* by combining the health score with content-modernity and AI-readiness signals.

Other quantitative outputs from the platform (cost allocation, gap analysis, scenario projection, peer benchmarking) are documented separately under `docs/methodologies/`.

Audience: University Council members, the President's executive team, deans, department heads, program coordinators, and senior analysts in the KKU Data Office. This paper does not assume a software-engineering background; it does assume familiarity with KKU's program structure (Faculty → Department → Program) and Thai higher-education context (TCAS, MHESI, the Buddhist-era academic calendar).

### 1.3 What this document does not do

- **It does not specify cost-allocation methodology.** The financial dimension consumes pre-computed `directCost` / `allocatedFacultyCost` / `allocatedFacilityCost` / `allocatedOverhead` values; how those values are produced is the subject of `docs/methodologies/cost-allocation.md` and is currently in draft (v0) pending Finance Committee approval.
- **It does not specify forecasting methodology.** The relevance dimension consumes 10-year demographic projections; the forecasting model that produces them is documented at `docs/methodologies/forecasting.md`.
- **It does not specify the AI peer review used by the lifecycle verdict's content-modernity gate.** That review is produced by a separate prompt-and-tool agent (`packages/shared/src/ai/prompts/program-peer-review.ts`); this paper consumes only its `verdict` output (`OLD_SCHOOL` / `IN_BETWEEN` / `FRONTIER`) and uses it as a categorical modifier as described in §3.5.

## 2. The Program Health Score

### 2.1 Overview

The Program Health Score is a **0–100 composite** computed at four scope levels (program, department, faculty, university) by averaging five **dimension scores**. Each dimension score is itself the average of two to four **driver scores**, each derived from a specific operational metric. Higher is healthier; the bands `< 40` (danger zone), `40–65` (needs attention), and `≥ 65` (healthy) are used in the user interface to signal severity, but no automatic action is triggered by any threshold.

The five dimensions are deliberately organised to map to the eight strategic questions in the project charter (Table 1).

**Table 1.** Mapping of health dimensions to strategic questions.

| Dimension | Charter question(s) | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Attractiveness** | Q1 — Do students choose this program? | Whether qualified applicants exist in sufficient number; whether they ultimately enrol. |
| **Financial** | Q2 — Does it pay for itself? | Revenue/cost balance and the trajectory of both. |
| **Capability** | Q6 — Is the teaching team strong and sustainable? | Faculty seniority, research output, and retirement horizon. |
| **Outcomes** | Q1, Q6 — Do graduates thrive? | Selectivity (TCAS minimum-score percentile), employment and salary post-graduation. |
| **Relevance** | Q1, Q3, Q7 — Is what is taught aligned with future demand? | Skill alignment with the labour market and demographic resilience. |

This structure is the result of a v3 redesign in May 2026 that replaced two previously placeholder dimensions (`relevance` and `capability`, both null in v2) with real-data backed scorers. Earlier versions are retained for audit; current computations stamp `health-score.v3.4`.

### 2.2 Five dimensions and their drivers

For each dimension, this section lists the drivers, the formula that produces each driver's 0–100 sub-score, and the data source. All formulas reproduce the live code; clamps to `[0, 100]` are applied implicitly to every driver.

#### 2.2.1 Attractiveness

The Attractiveness dimension answers: *Are students actively choosing this program over its alternatives, and does the choice translate into enrolment?* Inputs are TCAS rounds 1–4 funnel data plus the registrar's enrolment series.

| Driver | Formula | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| `tcas_competition_ratio` | Piecewise function of `applicants / enrollSeats`. ratio ≥ 10 → 100; 5 → 70; 2 → 40; 1 → 20; below 1 scales linearly to 0. | `tcas_admission_funnels` (latest **complete** entry-year, summed across rounds 1–4). |
| `tcas_fill_rate` | `min(passed / enrollSeats, 0.5) ÷ 0.5 × 100` | Same as above. |
| `tcas_registration_yield` | yield = `regReported / sendClearinghouse`. ≥ 0.85 → 100; 0.5 → 50; below 0.5 scales linearly to 0. | Same as above. |
| `tcas_competition_vs_peers` | `(kkuRatio / peerMedianRatio) × 50`, clamped. Score 50 = parity with the Thai peer median; 100 = 2× peer median. | KKU funnel above + `tcas_admission_stats` peer rows for matching subject signature, same year. |
| `enrollment_trend_slope` | Linear-regression slope of `enrolled` over the most recent 5 years (with a partial-current-year drop). 0 students/year → 50; +5/yr → 100; −5/yr → 0. | `enrollment_metrics` (KKU registrar, via `kku-registry`). |
| `kku_intake_policy` | Compliance with KKU's 2026 intake-floor policy: 30 students/yr for Bachelor's, 8 for postgraduate. Bachelor breach scales linearly toward 0; postgraduate breach scales toward 30. | `enrollment_metrics` + Program level. |

The "complete" entry-year filter is significant: in-progress recruitment cycles have null `regReported` values across all rounds (Clearinghouse registration finishes ~October of the cohort year), and including them would zero the registration-yield driver in a misleading way. The application uses the most recent **completed** cohort and surfaces the in-progress cycle separately as informational.

#### 2.2.2 Financial

The Financial dimension answers: *Does the program cover its operating costs, and is its revenue trajectory healthy?* It depends on the cost-allocation methodology (`docs/methodologies/cost-allocation.md`, currently v0 draft).

| Driver | Formula | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| `net_contribution_per_student` | `(tuition − allocatedTotalCost) / enrolled`, mapped: ≥ 100 000 THB → 100; 0 → 50; negative scales toward 0. | `financial_metrics` × `enrollment_metrics`, latest complete fiscal year. |
| `revenue_coverage_program_direct` | `tuition / (directCost + facilityCost + overhead)`. The government salary subsidy is **excluded** so the score reflects programme-level self-sufficiency. ≥ 1.0 → 80; ≥ 1.2 → 100. | `financial_metrics`. |
| `tuition_trend_slope` | Linear-regression slope of `tuitionRevenue` over the most recent 5 fiscal years. Mapped onto 0–100 around a 50 baseline. | `financial_metrics`. |

*Note on partial-year handling.* The Thai fiscal year (1 October–30 September) and the academic year (1 June–31 May) do not align. The financial scorer drops the most recent year if its values look partial (a heuristic: < 30% of the prior year's value), to avoid penalising programs for a not-yet-completed reporting cycle.

#### 2.2.3 Capability

The Capability dimension answers: *Does the program have a teaching team capable of delivering its curriculum today, and will it still have one in five years?*

| Driver | Formula | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| `senior_rank_share` | `(profCount + assocProfCount) / totalActive`, mapped so 50% senior share → 100. | `program_faculties` × `faculty_members.rank` (KKU HR). |
| `publications_per_faculty` | Count of `research_outputs` with `year ≥ currentYear − 3` divided by active faculty count, mapped so 5 outputs/faculty/year → 100. | `program_faculties` × `research_outputs` (KKU Research Affairs / OpenAlex). |
| `retirement_horizon_5y` | `100 − (countRetiringIn5y / totalActive × 100)`. More retirements due in the next five years → lower score. | `faculty_members.retirementDate`. |

#### 2.2.4 Outcomes

The Outcomes dimension answers: *How selective is admission, and how do graduates fare in the labour market?*

| Driver | Formula | Data source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tcas_min_score_percentile` | KKU programme's TCAS3 minimum admission score expressed as a percentile within its same-subject peer set, then mapped 0–100. | `tcas_admission_stats` (peer rows for matching subject, same year). | Live |
| `employment_rate` | `clamp(rate × 100)` | `graduate_outcomes` (alumni survey). | Pending — `kku-alumni` real connector |
| `average_salary` | `clamp(salary / 30 000 × 100)`; 30 000 THB/month → 100. | `graduate_outcomes`. | Pending |

When `kku-alumni` is in mocked mode, the latter two drivers contribute null values. The dimension score remains computable from the percentile alone, with `dataCompletenessPct ≈ 33` reflecting the gap.

#### 2.2.5 Relevance

The Relevance dimension answers: *Does what we teach match what the labour market — and the country's demographic future — will demand?*

| Driver | Formula | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| `labour_market_alignment` | Share of `program_skills` that intersect the demanded-skill set in the labour-market data table. A four-quadrant fallback (Thailand × Job postings → Global × Job postings → Thailand × Profiles → Global × Profiles) selects the most local signal available; the quadrant used is recorded in the score's raw payload. | `program_skills` ⨯ aggregated LinkedIn skill-demand data (data: **LinkedIn**; subscriber and licence holder: **MHESI**; API endpoint hosted by KMITL on behalf of MHESI). |
| `demand_match_filled` | `100 × (matchedDemanded / relevantDemanded)` — share of *demanded* skills the curriculum already teaches, restricted to the careers a curriculum is plausibly relevant to. | `program_demand_matches` (computed pre-analytics). |
| `demographic_resilience` | `100 − clamp(requiredShareAt+10y × 100 000 × 100)`. The required share is the fraction of Thailand's 18–22 cohort the programme would need to recruit from, ten years out, to maintain its current intake. < 0.05% → ≈ 100; > 0.2% → ≈ 0. | `programDemographicOutlook` (driven by Thailand cohort projections; see §4.3 for the calculation, §5 for the data source, and `docs/methodologies/forecasting.md`). |
| `strategic_sector_alignment` | 100 if any career mapped to the programme (via the labour-market career taxonomy used by the LinkedIn-derived data) intersects a Thai BCG (Bio / Circular / Green) or S-curve target sector; otherwise 50. | Career taxonomy from the labour-market data source × `Program` career mappings × strategic-sector heuristic. |

The `_kmitl` suffix on the internal driver name is a **stable code identifier** referencing the API endpoint and **must not be read as an attribution claim**. The data subject — LinkedIn-derived skill demand — is owned by LinkedIn. **MHESI** (the Ministry of Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation) holds the licence/subscription that makes the data accessible to Thai universities. **KMITL hosts the API endpoint on behalf of MHESI** but is neither the data owner nor the data curator. KKU consumes the data under MHESI's institutional partner programme. This attribution is reproduced in the provenance drawer of every chart that depends on the driver.

### 2.3 Aggregation and rollups

A program-scope health score is the equal-weighted average of the **non-null** dimension scores. Department, faculty, and university scopes are computed by aggregating program-level metrics first and then re-running the dimension scorers against the rolled-up totals — they are **not** averages of program scores.

This means a faculty's overall health score is **not** mathematically guaranteed to equal the weighted average of its programs' overall scores. The two are computed independently from the same underlying metrics. In practice the divergence is small, but the audit trail is what matters: each scope's score reproduces from the metrics observable at that scope on the day it was computed. A future version may enforce bottom-up consistency explicitly.

Where weighted-by-headcount aggregation is needed (e.g., outcome-family rollups), `enrolled` is the weight, so a 500-student programme moves the rollup more than a 12-student programme. This is consistent with the rollup rule in `packages/db/src/scope.ts`.

### 2.4 Null handling and data completeness

A null underlying metric is **excluded from the average**, never substituted with a 0 or a 50. The rationale is straightforward:

- Substituting 0 punishes the program for missing data ("we don't know your employment rate" would become "employment rate 0").
- Substituting 50 pretends we have neutral information when in fact we have none.
- Excluding the missing driver and surfacing an explicit `dataCompletenessPct` lets the reader see the real signal and form a calibrated view of how much to trust it.

Display rules in the user interface are non-negotiable:

- Completeness `< 50%` → score is rendered in tertiary-text colour and tooltipped "low data".
- Completeness `0%` → no score; cell shows `—`.
- Council briefs **must** mention completeness explicitly for any score they cite.

If every underlying driver in a dimension is null, the dimension score is null. If every dimension is null, the overall score is null. The composite never silently fabricates a value out of zero data.

### 2.5 Bands and decision-support framing

The application uses three bands for visualisation:

| Band | Score | Visual treatment | Decision implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Danger zone | `< 40` | Red | Triggers analyst review; does **not** trigger automatic action. |
| Needs attention | `40 ≤ x < 65` | Amber | Routine monitoring. |
| Healthy | `≥ 65` | Green | No flag. |

These bands are calibration choices, not thresholds with operational consequence. Council decisions on closure, merger, launch, or radical restructure require **two-reviewer human approval** under the project's AI governance policy (`docs/ai-governance.md` §human-in-the-loop) regardless of the computed score. The score's job is to surface candidates for review, not to authorise the review's conclusion.

## 3. The Program Lifecycle Verdict

### 3.1 Overview

The Lifecycle Verdict is a **four-band classifier** that combines the health score with two additional signals — content modernity and AI readiness — to express a *strategic trajectory* rather than an operational state.

**Table 2.** Verdict bands and their decision posture.

| Verdict | Trajectory score | Posture | Council action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sunset** | < 45 | Multiple signals declining; weak fundamentals; structural drift. | Closure, merger, or radical restructure on the table. |
| **Stable** | 45–65 | Meeting current expectations; not growing. | Hold and monitor. |
| **Rising** | 65–80 | Improving across multiple signals. | Fund continued development. |
| **Frontier** | ≥ 80, with `content_modernity = FRONTIER` | Leading-edge content with strong fundamentals. | Showcase and scale. |

The verdict is **rules-based, not learned.** Every contribution to the trajectory score and every modifier gate is auditable from the source code. An AI peer-review output is one input (and a critical one), but the rules over that input are deterministic and human-written.

### 3.2 Trajectory score: drivers and contributions

The trajectory score starts at a baseline of **50** and is adjusted by a weighted sum of nine **drivers**. Each driver's contribution is signed (lifting or dragging) and capped, so no single driver can move the verdict by more than its allotted weight. The final score is clamped to `[0, 100]`.

**Table 3.** Lifecycle drivers, weights, and what each measures.

| # | Driver | Weight cap | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `ai_readiness` | ±25 | AI-native skill share, AI-resilience of the curriculum, and AI-related publications by faculty. The single heaviest driver. |
| 2 | `health_overall` | ±20 | The program's overall health score (`(score − 50) × 0.4`). |
| 3 | `enrollment_trend_slope` | ±15 | Direction and steepness of recent enrolment trend. |
| 4 | `tuition_trend_slope` | ±10 | Direction of tuition revenue trend. |
| 5 | `content_modernity` | ±20 | AI peer-review verdict on curriculum content (`OLD_SCHOOL` / `IN_BETWEEN` / `FRONTIER`). |
| 6 | `major_content_gaps` | up to −12 | Penalty: 4 points per major curriculum gap identified by AI peer review, capped at 12. |
| 7 | `demographic_resilience` | ±10 | Programme's resilience to Thailand's 18–22 cohort decline. |
| 8 | `delayed_graduation_share` | up to −10 | Penalty for cohorts with high shares delayed past programme duration. |
| 9 | `capability_strength` | −8 to +8 | Faculty seniority and publication strength. |

The total possible movement from baseline is therefore approximately ±130 absolute, but clamping to `[0, 100]` and the asymmetric per-driver caps mean that no realistic combination produces a score outside `[0, 100]`. The contribution magnitudes were chosen to make AI signals (drivers 1, 5, 6) dominant, on the rationale that AI is the largest curriculum-relevant disruption Thai higher education faces this decade.

### 3.3 The AI-readiness sub-score

`ai_readiness` is itself a 0–100 composite computed from up to four sub-scores:

| Sub-score | Weight (4-driver / 3-driver) | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| `curriculumIntegration` | 0.40 / 0.44 | Weighted share of program skills classified as `aiNative`. 25% AI-native skills at typical weight produces ~100. |
| `skillResilience` | 0.30 / 0.33 | Balance of `aiNative + aiAugmented` against `aiDisplaceable` skills. 50 = neutral; > 50 = AI-resilient curriculum. |
| `researchPresence` | 0.20 / 0.23 | Share of recent (last 3 years) publications by program faculty whose titles match an AI/ML keyword set. |
| `pedagogicalReadiness` | 0.10 / — | *Reserved.* Currently null pending classroom and survey instrumentation; weight is redistributed equally across the other three when null. |

Skill classification (`aiNative` / `aiAugmented` / `aiDisplaceable` / `neutral`) is performed by a deterministic keyword match against the skill's English name; the keyword lists are committed in `packages/db/src/program-deep-dive.ts` and reviewed manually whenever the skill taxonomy is enlarged.

### 3.4 Verdict assignment

The verdict is assigned in two passes. **Pass 1** sets the verdict from the trajectory score alone:

```
score >= 80 AND content_modernity = FRONTIER     → FRONTIER
score >= 65                                        → RISING
score >= 45                                        → STABLE
otherwise                                          → SUNSET
```

**Pass 2** applies modifier gates (§3.5).

### 3.5 Modifier gates

Three gates can downgrade — but never upgrade — the Pass 1 verdict.

1. **Content modernity gate.** If the AI peer review's `contentModernity.verdict` is `OLD_SCHOOL`, any `RISING` or `FRONTIER` verdict is downgraded to `STABLE`. Rationale: a curriculum that the AI reviewer judges out of touch with the field cannot be called "rising" — its trajectory is illusory until content refreshes. (When the AI peer review has not yet run, this gate cannot apply.)
2. **AI-readiness floor (Stable cap).** If `ai_readiness < 30`, any `RISING` or `FRONTIER` verdict is downgraded to `STABLE`. Rationale: programs facing structural AI displacement risk should not be classified as growing on the basis of metrics that may not survive contact with the 2026+ labour market.
3. **AI-readiness floor (Frontier cap).** If `ai_readiness < 50`, a `FRONTIER` verdict is downgraded to `RISING`. Rationale: the FRONTIER label is reserved for genuinely AI-integrated programs.

These gates encode an explicit institutional position: that a high health score in a curriculum that is out of step with AI developments is, on the strategic horizon Council is considering, at risk regardless. Decision-makers may disagree with that position; the gates are documented openly so the disagreement is about the substance, not about an opaque algorithmic step.

### 3.6 Narrative

Every lifecycle output is accompanied by a 2–3 sentence rule-based narrative composed from the verdict band, the trajectory score, and the top two lifting and top two dragging drivers (sorted by absolute contribution). The narrative is **not** generated by a language model; it is templated. Where the AI peer review has not yet run for a program, the narrative says so explicitly.

### 3.7 Persistence and audit

Every lifecycle computation is upserted to a `program_lifecycle_snapshots` table with the verdict, trajectory score, methodology version (`lifecycle.v1.1` at time of writing), and the full driver payload as JSON. This enables (a) fast reads on the cockpit page without recomputation, and (b) a permanent audit trail of what verdict was assigned to which program on which date with which inputs. Snapshot-write failures are intentionally swallowed: the snapshot is observability, not source-of-truth.

## 4. Program Deep-Dive sectional methodology

The Program Deep-Dive page (`/programs/<id>`) is the per-program audit view used by deans, department heads, and program coordinators. It surfaces every signal the health score and lifecycle verdict consume, plus several presentation-only sections (faculty roster, peer set, Council decisions). This section documents the calculations specific to the Deep-Dive — those not already specified in §2 (health score) or §3 (lifecycle). Reference implementation: `packages/db/src/program-deep-dive.ts`.

### 4.1 Demand funnel — cohort lifecycle

The demand funnel reconstructs a single cohort's journey through the program: applied → admitted → enrolled → graduated → other terminal states (renounced, no-report, dropped, transferred). Inputs:

- TCAS rows (`tcas_admission_stats`) for the applied / admitted counts.
- `student_cohort_distributions` for currently-enrolled students.
- `alumni_cohort_status` (`AlumniCohortStatus`) for graduated and dropped/renounced/transferred buckets.

The funnel anchors on the **most recent completed cohort**: `currentBE − durationYears − 1`. This is the last entry-year that should already have produced on-time graduates; including more recent cohorts would make graduation rates look lower than they really are.

**Status buckets shown to readers** (alumni-side):

| Bucket | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Graduated | Conferred a degree. |
| Renounced | Voluntary cancellation by the student. |
| No-report | Registrar has no current status; cohort stale. |
| Dropped — financial | Removed for non-payment. |
| Dropped — academic | Removed for academic standing. |
| Dropped — voluntary | Other voluntary exit not classified above. |
| Transferred | Moved to another program. |
| Still enrolled | Active in the cohort distribution table. |

The funnel exposes raw counts only; conversion rates (e.g. % graduated of admitted) are computed in the Deep-Dive UI from the same anchor cohort. When TCAS rows exist for the same year at multiple rounds, the *highest* round is used (round 3.2 supersedes 3.1, etc.).

### 4.2 Cohort distribution — currently active students

The Cohort Distribution panel partitions today's active students by **entry year** and assigns each a **year-in-programme** and a **status** of `incoming`, `on-track`, or `delayed`. It reads from `StudentCohortDistribution` (one row per program × entry year).

The current academic year (BE) is computed from the system clock: months ≥ June use `currentCE + 543`; January–May use `currentCE + 543 − 1` (the Thai academic year starts in June). Year-in-programme = `currentAcademicYearBE − entryYearBE + 1`. Status:

- `incoming` if year-in-programme ≤ 0 (cohort hasn't started classes yet);
- `delayed` if year-in-programme > program duration;
- `on-track` otherwise.

The `delayed` count feeds the Lifecycle Verdict's `delayed_graduation_share` driver (§3.2). The status field never has a "graduated" value because a graduated cohort has been removed from the active distribution by the registrar's data pipeline.

### 4.3 Demographic outlook — required-share-of-cohort projection

The demographic outlook projects the **share of Thailand's catchment cohort** the program would need to recruit from at +0, +5, +10, and +15 years to maintain today's first-year intake. Bachelor's programmes use the **18–22 cohort**; postgraduate programmes use the **23–29 cohort**. The catchment is **Northeast Thailand** by default for KKU programmes (an institutional choice — the cohort projection table also carries national figures for cross-checks).

Pseudocode for the required-share at horizon `h`:

```
requiredShare(h) = baselineYearOneIntake / cohortPopulation(now + h)
```

`baselineYearOneIntake` is the most recent **non-partial** Year-1 enrolment (the partial-year drop heuristic from §2.2.2 applies). The risk verdict is taken from the +10y required-share, calibrated for KKU's typical programme size:

| Required share at +10y (Northeast 18–22) | Risk verdict |
|---|---|
| < 0.05% | `low` |
| 0.05%–0.20% | `medium` |
| > 0.20% | `high` |

These thresholds are **per-programme** — at the institutional aggregate (sum across all KKU bachelor programmes), the same calculation produces the institutional demographic ceiling, surfaced separately at the Cockpit / portfolio level. The projection source is pinned to `NESDC-PopProj-2010-2040` so older synthetic seed rows do not dilute the read.

### 4.4 Skill-match score — curriculum vs labour-market demand

The Deep-Dive's Skills section shows three lists side by side: **taught** (program skills, ranked PLO-tagged first), **demanded** (labour-market signal), and **gaps** (demanded but not taught). It also reports a single composite **match score** in `[0, 100]`.

**Demand resolution** uses a four-tier fallback chain, recorded transparently in the response so analysts know which scope produced the read:

1. Thailand × Job postings (percentage ≥ 5%);
2. Thailand × Profiles (percentage ≥ 5%);
3. Global × Job postings (percentage ≥ 0%);
4. Global × Profiles (percentage ≥ 0%).

Engineering and specialty careers often have no Thailand × Job postings rows because the Thai job-postings sample is thin and skewed toward digital/service careers; the global fallbacks ensure the panel always has signal. The label of the tier that succeeded is shown in the UI so readers apply the right caveat.

**Career filtering.** When a programme has `kmitlCareerIds` set, demand rows are restricted to those careers — a Mechanical Engineering programme sees Mechanical Engineer / Mechanical Design Engineer / Mechanical Project Engineer demand, not generic top-skills across all 401 careers. Programmes without a career mapping fall back to all careers with a UI banner noting the looser scope.

**Match score formula.** When `program_demand_matches` (the AI-computed demand-match table) has rows for the program, the score uses **only relevant** demanded skills as the denominator, so the score reflects whether the curriculum covers what its graduates would actually need:

```
matchScore = 100 × (matchedRelevant / totalRelevant)
```

Without AI matches, the score falls back to a taxonomy-only intersection: `100 × (taughtIds ∩ demandIds) / |demandIds|`. The fallback is conservative and the UI flags it so the analyst knows to run `pnpm programs:match-demand`.

The data source attribution chain for the demand side is the same as in §2.2.5: **LinkedIn data, subscribed by MHESI, hosted at KMITL on behalf of MHESI.**

### 4.5 AI peer review — content-modernity verdict and content gaps

The AI peer review is generated by a separate prompt-and-tool agent (`packages/shared/src/ai/prompts/program-peer-review.ts`) and persisted on the `ProgramPeerReview` table. The Deep-Dive consumes:

- **Strengths and weaknesses** — bullet list (claim + evidence);
- **Tier verdicts** (WORLD / ASIA / SOUTHEAST_ASIA / THAILAND_PEER) — one-line positioning per tier;
- **Content modernity** — `OLD_SCHOOL` | `IN_BETWEEN` | `FRONTIER` plus a narrative;
- **Content gaps** — list of topics where KKU lags peers, each with severity (`major` | `moderate` | `minor`), KKU status, peer examples, and a recommendation;
- **Recommendations** — prioritised actions (`high` | `medium` | `low`) with effort horizons (`short` | `medium` | `long`);
- **Confidence** — model's self-rated `[0, 1]`;
- **Subject benchmark summary** — narrative paragraph.

The Lifecycle Verdict (§3) consumes only `contentModernity.verdict` and the count of `severity = major` content gaps. The richer fields are presentation-only on the Deep-Dive. AI peer-review outputs below the auto-approve confidence threshold (currently 0.85, configurable) sit in an analyst review queue and are never silently materialised — see §6 (Governance).

### 4.6 Faculty roster — visibility and role-gating

The Faculty section's roster fallback chain:

1. `ProgramFaculty` — curated per-program links (analyst-driven; not populated by any connector today);
2. Department-scoped — all `FacultyMember` rows where `departmentId = program.departmentId`;
3. Faculty-scoped — all `FacultyMember` rows where `facultyId = program.facultyId`.

In practice most programmes today land at the faculty scope until an analyst attaches a department or per-program roster.

**Visibility** is role-gated and enforced at the database query layer (not just filtered post-fetch):

| Visibility | Who sees it | Fields exposed |
|---|---|---|
| `full` | DEAN / DEPT_HEAD / ANALYST / ADMIN / EXECUTIVE | All fields including `nameEn`, `nameTh`, `employeeCode`, `specialization`, `hireDate`. |
| `aggregates-only` | COUNCIL | Rank distribution, retirement horizon, qualification, research-output count, active flag. **No** identifying personal fields. |

The `aggregates-only` mode lets Council members read the program-level capability signal without seeing individuals — an explicit PDPA posture choice. The same gating extends to any future personal-data fields (e.g., compensation): adding such a field selects `false` in the aggregates-only `select` block so the wrong data never leaves the database.

### 4.7 Persistence and freshness

Deep-Dive sections have no per-program-snapshot table (unlike the Lifecycle Verdict at §3.7); each section reads from its source tables on demand. The **freshness timestamp** displayed at the top of every Deep-Dive section is the most recent `IngestionRun.completedAt` across the underlying sources for that section. `<ProvenanceButton>` opens the canonical Provenance drawer.

## 6. Governance, audit, and versioning

### 6.1 Versioning policy

The methodology version constants are:

- `HEALTH_SCORE_VERSION = 'health-score.v3.4'` (in `packages/db/src/health-score.ts`).
- `LIFECYCLE_VERSION = 'lifecycle.v1.1'` (in `packages/db/src/program-deep-dive.ts`).

Every score row emitted by the application carries the version that produced it. Historical scores are **not** retroactively backfilled when the version is bumped; they remain stamped with the version current at the time of computation.

A version is bumped when, and only when, a substantive change occurs to the formula, the data sources, the null-handling rules, the completeness thresholds, or the verdict bands. Cosmetic changes (rephrasing labels, fixing typos in narrative templates) do not bump the version. The companion document at `docs/methodologies/health-score.md` records every bump and the reasoning behind it.

### 6.2 Audit and reproducibility

Every score and verdict is reproducible from:

1. The git commit recorded in the `programLifecycleSnapshots.driversJson` payload (or, for health scores, the `IngestionRun` rows referenced in provenance);
2. The metric rows in the database at the time of computation;
3. The methodology version stamp.

Council Pack PDFs include a methodology-version footer on every page. A score cited in a Council brief can be traced — value, formula, and inputs — to its commit.

### 6.3 Human-in-the-loop requirement

No score, verdict, or AI-generated narrative authorises an institutional decision. Per `docs/ai-governance.md`:

- Program closure, merger, or launch requires **two-reviewer Council approval** with explicit human sign-off.
- AI-generated extractions (skills, course descriptions, narratives) below a configurable confidence threshold (currently `0.85`) sit in an analyst review queue and are never silently materialised.
- Disagreements raised by deans or program coordinators are persisted as `Annotation` rows linked to the specific AI inference, enabling subsequent recalibration.

### 6.4 Guardrails enforced at compute time

The scoring service enforces, at every computation:

- All driver and dimension scores are clamped to `[0, 100]`.
- `dataCompletenessPct ∈ [0, 100]`.
- If every underlying metric is null, the score returned is `null` — never `0`.
- The methodology version is stamped on every returned row.

These are coded invariants, not policy aspirations.

## 7. Limitations and known caveats

This section is deliberately exhaustive. Decision-makers should read it with care.

### 7.1 Equal-weighting is a deliberate, conservative default

The composite score weighs all five dimensions equally. This is a least-arguable default: any weighted formulation encodes a value judgement, and the application errs toward making the unweighted reading available so decision-makers form their own bias rather than inherit ours. Should a future revision adopt weights, those weights will be visible in chart tooltips, in the Council Pack methodology footer, and in this document.

### 7.2 Data completeness varies sharply across programs

A non-trivial number of KKU programs lack complete data for one or more dimensions:

- The Outcomes dimension is largely null pending the `kku-alumni` real connector and PDPA sign-off; only the TCAS minimum-score percentile is currently live.
- Thirty of the 297 KKU programmes carry no current-curriculum PDF on disk and therefore have no extracted-skill record — their Relevance dimension is null.
- The cost-allocation methodology is in v0 draft and the Financial dimension's cost-side numbers are preview-quality.

Where completeness is low, the user interface flags it explicitly. Council briefs that cite scores must report completeness alongside the score.

### 7.3 Cross-scope consistency is not asserted

A faculty's overall health score is computed independently from its own scope's metric rollups, not as an average of its programs' scores. The two are typically close but not identical. A future version may enforce bottom-up consistency by construction; the current version trades that property for compute simplicity and per-scope auditability.

### 7.4 Some signals are still synthetic or proxied

- The labour-market alignment driver pulls from the LinkedIn-derived data feed (subscribed by MHESI; API hosted at KMITL) and is, until 2026-05-31, in a 30-day green-history readiness window. Council-facing surfaces should mark it as preview-quality.
- The `pedagogicalReadiness` sub-score in AI readiness is reserved (null) pending classroom and survey instrumentation; its weight is currently redistributed across the other three sub-scores.
- The `delayed_graduation_share` driver assumes the program's `durationYears` field is set correctly; programs with an incorrect duration will produce a misleading delayed-share signal.

### 7.5 The lifecycle verdict's modifier gates are an institutional position, not an empirical fact

The two AI-readiness floors (§3.5) and the OLD_SCHOOL content cap (§3.5) are explicit institutional positions, not findings. Decision-makers may disagree with those positions; the gates are documented openly so disagreement is about substance.

### 7.6 The verdict bands are calibration choices

The trajectory cut-offs (45 / 65 / 80) and the health bands (40 / 65) were chosen to produce a roughly even distribution of programs across bands at KKU's current state. They are not derived from external benchmark data. As more peer-comparison data lands, future revisions may recalibrate; this paper will be re-issued accordingly.

### 7.7 Score volatility under data refreshes

A single ingestion run can move a program's overall score by several points if it lands a previously-missing driver. This is by design (we want fresh data to register). Users of the cockpit are advised to compare scores across **versioned methodology stamps**, not across raw timestamps, when assessing trend.

## 8. Glossary

- **CUPT** — Council of University Presidents of Thailand (สมาคมที่ประชุมอธิการบดีแห่งประเทศไทย); administers TCAS.
- **TCAS** — Thai University Central Admission System; the four-round national admission process for Thai universities. KKU participates in rounds 1–4.
- **MHESI** — Ministry of Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation (Thailand). Sets national policy for higher education and research.
- **NSO** — National Statistical Office (Thailand). Source for population and labour-force statistics.
- **NESDC** — Office of the National Economic and Social Development Council (Thailand).
- **NXPO** — Office of National Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation Policy Council. Publishes national strategy documents including S-curve and BCG sector frameworks.
- **EECO** — Eastern Economic Corridor Office.
- **PDPA** — Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act (พระราชบัญญัติคุ้มครองข้อมูลส่วนบุคคล), 2019.
- **BCG model** — Bio-Circular-Green economic model, a Thai government strategic framework.
- **S-curve sectors** — Thailand's twelve targeted industries under the country's industrial-upgrading strategy.
- **OpenAlex** — A free, open catalogue of scholarly works, authors, and institutions, operated by OurResearch (non-profit).
- **Health-score driver** — A single 0–100 sub-score that contributes to one health-score dimension.
- **Lifecycle driver** — A signed contribution to the trajectory score; capped to a per-driver maximum.
- **Modifier gate** — A rule that can downgrade a verdict from a higher band to a lower one based on a categorical input.
